There's one thing about the Guiyou edition of The Story of the Stone that I find most perplexing, and that shocks many readers, and that's also the reason why people support it.I wonder if any readers have noticed that the Guiyou edition has a certain characteristic in terms of writing style. It's sometimes good and sometimes bad.Especially in the Guiyou edition, where political discourse is published, the writing style suddenly becomes excellent. However, once the political discourse departs, the descriptions of trivial matters become quite ordinary.Let me give you a few examples.He slowly said, "Throughout history, year after year, how tragic and similar it has been. Even with lessons from the past and warnings, it is difficult to overcome the rebellious and treacherous hearts of people. It is a place of life and death, a path of survival and destruction. A wise man is buried under a crumbling wall; who can hide there? Since everyone fears war, why would anyone want to go there?"Daiyu sighed, "When things are injustice, people cry out. Since ancient times, the king's rule has been awe-inspiring, but he has rarely shown compassion for the suffering of the people. It is as if the fate of the entire country rests solely on his own, and the lives of countless ordinary people are as insignificant as mustard seeds. But this is not the case. The people are the most important, and the ruler is the least important. Now, bandits are rampant on all sides, the world is in disarray, the king's authority is weak, the court and the people are filled with fear, the state is on the verge of collapse, and the army is at the gates of the city. This is not entirely the fault of the bandits, but the use of violence to control power has endless drawbacks. The villains are inherently scoundrels and wicked people. They pretend to believe in righteousness and join the bandits. They appear to be willing to obey their leaders, but in reality, they are just enduring and suppressing their anger. They are merely taking the opportunity to rise to a higher position and carry out mass killings." "If a villain were to rule the world, his corrupt character would surely reveal his true, ruthless nature, eliminating his generals and commanders. His so-called 'righteousness' and 'benevolence' would be nothing but lies. If a villain were to establish a new dynasty, repeating the same mistakes, the people would suffer greatly, and sooner or later, the people would rebel, their lives ending in ruin. Dynasties have changed, but the essence remains the same; when have the people ever truly enjoyed wealth and peace? When the rulers act unjustly, the people will surely follow suit. If a ruler seizes power by force, proclaiming 'it is only natural,' the people will inevitably learn from him. If he seizes power again by force, teaching the people with wickedness, how can the people remain righteous?" The bandits sneered, "Stop wasting your breath! It's the emperor who forced us to rebel. He is unrighteous, unjust, and corrupt, only knowing how to indulge in debauchery, seize by force, and kill indiscriminately. The people are suffering, starving and destitute; they'll starve to death anyway, so we might as well rebel and seize the land!"Originally, these were Tan Chun's indignant words written before her departure."I, born of a concubine, of lowly birth, have long harbored ambitions to rule the country. I have witnessed many of your shortcomings as rulers. Now, having been married off to a distant land, unlikely to return, I dare to point out the ills of our nation: A ruler manages his household with countless offspring, some intelligent, some foolish, some healthy, some simple-minded. Whom to pass the throne to is of paramount importance. Even a foolish or dull-witted son is not to be disregarded; a son born of a concubine, intelligent, capable, loyal, and brave, is ignored simply because of his low rank. The nation is treated like a child's game! The struggle for succession and power is a bloodbath among descendants, familial bonds are ruthless, harboring malicious intentions, imprisoning relatives, imprisoning brothers—a scene of carnage and tragedy! Many children bring many hardships; why have multiple wives? Ruler and ministers are mutually suspicious, their hearts constantly in turmoil. The ruler worries about losing his throne, the ministers worry about their lives." A family should appoint virtuous and capable officials, trusting them without doubt. If suspicion lingered, why bestow titles in the first place? The emperor and his ministers scheme against each other, officials oppress the common people, all talk of benefiting the people, yet the imperial cause is wasted! Next year, righteous armies will rise from all sides, heroes will run rampant, the emperor will be in a panic, and tears will flow like a river in spring. The wise and capable could still manage the chaos and order, and were willing to exert their utmost efforts, but they were all killed one by one, leaving the court and the country without anyone capable. Treacherous ministers, having been appointed without hesitation, flatter the emperor, and the foolish emperor's favor is overwhelming, unaware that disaster will befall the capital! Who knew that the great way would be lost, and the royal family would be utterly destroyed? When will there be a chance for future prosperity? I now leave my family and loved ones, board a boat and travel thousands of miles away, from now on we will be separated by the ends of the earth, and may we never meet again in this life!When it comes to "discussing politics," four-character idioms like these come naturally and are easy to pronounce.Is this really the subconscious writing style of a modern person? You're saying this was written by He Lili ?If you insist that this was written by He Lili, I'll support you. But how do you explain the sudden drop in quality when He Lili writes about ordinary, trivial matters?Even if we take a step back and consider the writing style, the writing styles for political discourse and those for trivial matters are completely different. One uses four-character idioms like crazy, while the other seems to be written in an assembly line style. There should at least be an explanation, right?Looking at the first eighty chapters, there are similar political discussions.When Baoyu was talking animatedly and saw that he had stopped, he laughed and said, "Everyone dies, as long as they die well. Those filthy men only know how to die for their country, whether it's to remonstrate with the emperor or to fight for their country. These two kinds of deaths are for a true man to die for his reputation and integrity. But how can they compare to not dying at all? They will surely remonstrate with a foolish emperor, only caring about gaining fame and risking their lives. Where will they leave the emperor in the future? They will surely fight with swords and spears, only caring about gaining fame and risking their lives. Where will they leave the country in the future? So these are not true deaths."Even Lin Daiyu remarked, "It's truly a case of 'tigers and wolves lurking on the steps, yet still discussing cause and effect.'"Upon hearing this, Baoyu , a scholar of *Dream of the Red Chamber* who was horrified, was overjoyed and quickly said with a smile, "That's excellent! I've often seen officials and people following foreign captives to present as tribute, drawn by their bravery in the face of wind and frost and their ease with horseback riding. Moreover, these two groups have been a scourge to China since the time of Yao and Shun , causing immense suffering to the Jin and Tang dynasties. Fortunately, we are blessed to be born in this era, as direct descendants of the Great Shun, and the virtuous and filial deeds of the sage Yu are illustrious, their fame reaching the heavens, immortal as the sun and moon. Therefore, all the clowns who ran rampant in previous dynasties now, without a single battle, have been forced by Heaven to bow their heads and surrender from afar. We should humiliate them and bring honor to our sovereign."Every single word of these was written by the "Cao Cao" you all talk about. You pretended not to see the first eighty chapters when he wrote these things, but when he wrote about politics in the last twenty-eight chapters, you said it was too vulgar.I wonder if anyone has studied He Lili's original version and the latest version from 2020.The two versions changed a lot of things, but almost all of them were changes to the beginning of the text; the later versions had fewer changes.The latter part of the 20th edition is almost identical to the latter part of the original edition.Can I make a bold guess?The version released by He Lili in 2008 was based on her revisions of the original text from the very beginning. She only published what she wanted to publish, even adding various embellishments.Later on, He Lili got tired of revising and simply released the original text. That's why the later chapters of the Guiyou edition are particularly memorable.That's why many people say they "stopped reading after a few chapters." Of course, He Lili must have revised a lot of things in the early stages.And the framework of the Guiyou edition.Those familiar with classical Chinese novels should know that there's a pattern: they often begin with a story, then the protagonist experiences a change in fortune—either good or bad—and the author then imparts a moral lesson. Finally, at the end, the protagonist has a profound realization, understanding that life is but a dream.The Three Words and Two Slaps, The Words to Awaken the World, Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio...Look at "Jin Ping Mei". The beginning is full of scheming and intrigue between people, and the coldness and warmth of human relationships. The ending is still about Wu Yueniang having a dream, realizing the truth, and then willingly letting Ximen Xiaoge become a monk to leave behind the mortal world.At the end of "Jin Ping Mei", the Northern Song Dynasty becomes the Southern Song Dynasty, and Dai'an changes his name to Ximen An and inherits Ximen Qing's family business.In the ending of the Guiyou edition , the Ming Dynasty becomes the Qing Dynasty, and Jia Lan and Jia Jun, descendants of the Jia family, become the new Dukes.The *Guiyou Manuscript* describes a sky full of cold, bustling stars that pierce the soul, evoking the myriad aspects of life, the joys and sorrows of separation and reunion, birth, aging, sickness, and death, and the fragility of human relationships. A sense of desolation washes over him, accompanied by the incessant chirping of insects. Baoyu thinks of his family scattered and dead, a vast expanse of white bones piled high on the earth—a cleansing sight indeed. Tears stream down his face, but grief is futile. Spring returns to summer, autumn to winter, the vast sky—where does love find its home? A mere speck of dust, vanishing in an instant—what is sorrow, what is suffering? Now he understands. What is fame, what is profit? All are self-inflicted troubles. Better to forget the mortal world, live a life of blissful ignorance, endure the passing years, and die one day, free from all suffering, turning to ashes and smoke.Jia Baoyu suddenly had a profound awakening, understanding that "fame and fortune are all illusions, and life is but a dream."What was Jia Baoyu's situation at this time?His family fell into decline, all his relatives died, and his lover also died. His only "lover," Xue Baochai, kept pushing him to study, and he left Xue Baochai.Even after experiencing so many setbacks and shocks, Jia Baoyu still hadn't achieved a complete enlightenment at this point.He only truly understood things after experiencing life as a businessman, a wanderer, a monk, a farmer, a vagrant, and a beggar.Even after the death of his only remaining relative, Shi Xiangyun, Jia Baoyu could no longer bear it and felt that his time was coming to an end. Finally, in a scene of "one person, one boat, one day. On the vast sea, without wind or waves. In the distance, the sun rises, and he feels as insignificant as a speck of dust," he reflected on his life.How many triumphs and tribulations has a person experienced, how much desolation and bitterness has a person tasted throughout their life?I had a dream where I entered the Land of Illusion, was pulled into the water by a water ghost, and then returned to reality.The ending takes place in reality; he jumps into the sea to commit suicide, but is pulled to the surface by King Kong and returned to the Land of Illusion.Reality – Dream – Illusion – Reality – IllusionYou say these ninety years in this world are real? Jia Baoyu's entire life seems like a grand dream.Let me ask you this: isn't this authentic classical culture, the kind of flavor found in classical novels?I believe the author was an ancient person who survived the late Ming and early Qing dynasties, experiencing one of the most chaotic periods in China's thousands of years of history.Having experienced countless instances of the coldness and warmth of human relationships, and witnessed far too much killing.The author hates the powerful and wealthy officials of the Ming Dynasty and regrets the Ming Dynasty's demise and occupation by foreign tribes.You must understand, the Jia family was a place where "besides the stone lions, there was nothing clean." The Jia family was the one that rode on the backs of ordinary people, lording it over them.The author also observed that dynastic changes did not alter the fate of ordinary people. When the country was prosperous, the people had to pay various taxes; when the country was in turmoil, the people suffered as well. For the past two thousand years, China seemed to have been stagnant, with dynasties constantly changing. The author felt utterly despair. So many dynasties before the Ming Dynasty, so much history to study, so many lessons to learn from, yet they still all headed towards destruction.On a small scale, it represents the downfall of a family; on a larger scale, it represents the downfall of a nation. It explicitly depicts the family, but implicitly reflects the fate of the nation.If the root causes are not changed, even if one such family or country is destroyed, countless others will continue the same pattern and perish.Read Gao E's sequel."Zhongxiang Kui Baoyu relinquishes worldly ties, and the Jia family enjoys imperial favor and its blessings continue."They really know how to fawn over someone.Everyone, ask yourselves honestly, has the Jia family ever done a single good deed?The entire Jia family was exploiting the common people.
